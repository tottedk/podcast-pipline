### Heidegger, B&T §§35-38 (everyday disclosedness and falling)
| § | Pages (H.) | Core |
|---|---|---|
| intro B | 210-211 (H. 167) | The analysis is "purely ontological" and "far removed from any moralizing critique of everyday Dasein" (211, H. 167). |
| 35 Idle talk (Gerede) | 211-214 (H. 167-170) | Not meant disparagingly. Communication drifts from the thing talked about to "what is said-in-the-talk"; gossiping and passing the word along; "Things are so because one says so" (212, H. 168). Extends to writing ("scribbling", superficial reading). Understanding everything "without previously making the thing one's own" (213, H. 169). Closes off rather than discloses; discourages new inquiry. We grow into this interpretedness and never fully extricate ourselves; all genuine understanding happens "in it, out of it, and against it" (213, H. 169). The "they" prescribes one's state-of-mind (213, H. 170). |
| 36 Curiosity (Neugier) | 214-217 (H. 170-173) | Aristotle's "desire to know" read as desire to see; Augustine's "lust of the eyes". Freed from work, circumspection seeks the far away just for its looks. Curiosity "seeks novelty only in order to leap from it anew to another novelty" (216, H. 172); not tarrying, distraction, "never dwelling anywhere"; "Curiosity is everywhere and nowhere" (217, H. 173). Idle talk says what one "must" have read and seen. |
| 37 Ambiguity (Zweideutigkeit) | 217-219 (H. 173-175) | Impossible to decide what is genuinely understood. Everyone has "scented out" in advance; interest dies once something is actually done; idle talk "lives at a faster rate" than real work (218, H. 174); a new creation is out of date as soon as it appears. Being-with in the "they" is "an intent, ambiguous watching of one another"; "Under the mask of 'for-one-another', an 'against-one-another' is in play" (219, H. 175). |
| 38 Falling (Verfallen) and thrownness | 219-224 (H. 175-180) | Falling = absorption in the "world" and in the "they"; "does not express any negative evaluation" (220, H. 175); not a fall from a purer "primal status" (220, H. 176); not a flaw that advanced cultures could get rid of (220, H. 176). ## 2. Key points by theme
**A. Who am I, really? (Heidegger §25-27)**
- The "I" is not the obvious starting point; proximally Dasein is not itself (150-152, H. 115-116).
- We are always already with Others, even when alone; loneliness is a mode of Being-with (157, H. 120).
- The "they" is nobody in particular and everybody; "Everyone is the other, and no one is himself" (165, H. 128).
- It works through public life: "In utilizing public means of transport and in making use of information services such as the newspaper, every Other is like the next" (164, H. 126).
- "We take pleasure and enjoy ourselves as they take pleasure; we read, see, and judge about literature and art as they see and judge" (164, H. 126-127; "they" = man).
- Averageness and levelling down: "Every kind of priority gets noiselessly suppressed" (165, H. 127).
- Disburdening: the "they" takes away answerability; "it was no one" (165, H. 127).
- The "they" is an existentiale, part of Dasein's positive constitution; its dominion may change in the course of history (167, H. 129).
- "Proximally Dasein is 'they', and for the most part it remains so" (167, H. 129).
- Authenticity is an existentiell modification of the "they", not a detachment from it (168, H. 130).

**B. Talk, curiosity, ambiguity (§35-37)** - see outline; key for social media analogies (Heidegger obviously wrote in 1927 about newspapers, gossip and "scribbling", not about the internet; any modern application is our interpretation).

**C. Falling is not a moral judgement - or is it? (§38, intro B)**
- Heidegger repeatedly denies a negative evaluation (211, H. 167; 220, H. 175-176; 224, H. 180).
- Yet the vocabulary (idle talk, uprooted, groundless, plunge, tempting, alienating) carries obvious evaluative weight: this tension is the antagonist's opening.

**D. Achievement society (Han)** - Should -> Can; self-exploitation; depression and burnout as diseases of too much positivity; "Yes, we can".

**E. Attention and rest (Han)** - hyperattention vs deep boredom; negative potency; the Sabbath; Handke's shared tiredness.

**F. Links Heidegger <-> Han**
- Heidegger's "hustle" [Betrieb] of falling (222, H. 177-178) and curiosity's restlessness (216, H. 172) prefigure Han's hyperactivity and hyperattention (12-13, 22). Connection is our interpretation.
- Difference: Heidegger's "they" is conformity to Others; Han's achievement-subject is "subject to no one" except itself (11, 46). Han says the disappearance of the commanding Other is the problem (38).
- Han is critical of Heidegger in places: Arendt's "heroic actionism, like her teacher Heidegger" (16); Heidegger's time defined by care and work vs the Sabbath of not-to (34).

## 4. Antagonist A: Adorno, *The Jargon of Authenticity* (NOT in books; paraphrase only, no quotes)
Verifiable, well-established points only:
- Theodor W. Adorno, *Jargon der Eigentlichkeit* (1964); English *The Jargon of Authenticity*, tr. Knut Tarnowski and Frederic Will (Northwestern University Press, 1973).
- Target: a postwar West German "jargon" of authenticity, an elevated vocabulary (words such as encounter, commitment, genuine, existential) used in philosophy, theology, pedagogy and public speech that sounds profound and gives words an aura of meaning independent of their content. Adorno treats it as ideology.
- Heidegger is the central philosophical target (alongside Jaspers and others); Adorno argues that Heidegger's concept of authenticity and the critique of the "they" turn historically produced social conditions (alienation, the pressures of modern work and administration) into timeless structures of human existence, which shields those conditions from social criticism.
- Adorno charges that the existential vocabulary, despite its anti-conformist pose, itself becomes a conformist, marketable language, and that its contempt for the everyday "they" carries an elitist tone.
- Context: Adorno, with Horkheimer, wrote the critique of the "culture industry" in *Dialectic of Enlightenment* (1944/1947). So Adorno is no friend of mass culture either: his quarrel is that the critique must be social and historical, not ontological. (Useful concession for both sides.)
- Context, widely documented: Heidegger joined the Nazi party in 1933 and was rector of Freiburg University 1933-34; the *Black Notebooks* (published from 2014) intensified debate about antisemitism in his thought. Adorno connects the jargon with authoritarian traditions in German thought. Keep this brief and factual in the script.

## 5. Antagonist B: the tech / liberal optimist (paraphrase only, no quotes)
- Steven Pinker, *Enlightenment Now: The Case for Reason, Science, Humanism, and Progress* (2018): with data, argues that life has improved on most measures (life expectancy, extreme poverty, violence, literacy, leisure). He reports that working hours in rich countries have fallen substantially since the 19th century and that household technology freed time. He criticises intellectuals' habit of declinism ("progressophobia" is his term, used as a label only). He argues that life satisfaction tends to be higher in richer countries and that reports of an epidemic of depression and anxiety are complicated by wider diagnosis and reporting (phrase cautiously).
- The general connectivity argument: networked tools lower the cost of finding like-minded people and organising collective action (e.g. Clay Shirky, *Here Comes Everybody*, 2008; *Cognitive Surplus*, 2010). For people who are isolated or in minorities, online communities can be a lifeline. This is the general argument; no specific statistics are used.
- WHO (ICD-11, 2019): burnout is classified as an "occupational phenomenon" resulting from chronic workplace stress that has not been successfully managed, not as a medical condition. Useful for "is burnout philosophy or just a bad boss?". (Verifiable public classification; short term "occupational phenomenon" may be cited as the WHO's term.)

## 6. Defenders (for the advocate)
- Heidegger himself pre-empts the moralism charge (211, H. 167; 220, H. 175-176; 224, H. 180) and says authenticity is a modification of everydayness, not an escape to an elite (168, H. 130; 224, H. 179).
- "Leaping ahead" solicitude (159, H. 122) shows Heidegger has a positive model of relating to others.
- Han answers the "bad boss" objection: the point is that there is no boss; compulsion has moved inside (11, 19, 46-49).
- Han answers the optimist: freedom and constraint coincide; more options and more activity are not more freedom (11, 22).
