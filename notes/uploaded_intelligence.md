**About *Pantheon* (basic premise only, no spoilers):** an animated series (AMC, 2022) developed by Craig Silverstein from Ken Liu's stories. A bullied teenager, Maddie, gets help from an anonymous online presence that turns out to be her dead father, David, whose brain was scanned and turned into an "Uploaded Intelligence" (UI). Anything beyond this premise counts as a spoiler and stays out of the script. In the stories the father's name is David and the company is Logorhythms; I did not check whether the show uses the same names, so the script says only "her dad" and "a tech company".

## 1. Outline
**Chalmers (2010), uploading sections**
- Kinds of uploading: gradual, instant and delayed; destructive, nondestructive and reconstructive. The examples are serial sectioning of a frozen brain (destructive) and "nanotransfer", which replaces neurons one at a time (gradual) (§8, pp.33-34).
- The key question splits in two: "will an uploaded version of me be conscious? Second, will it be me?" (p.34).
- **Consciousness (§9):** biological theorists (Searle and Block are named as sympathetic) vs functionalists (Dennett and Chalmers). The fading/sudden-disappearance argument: if neurons are replaced one by one with perfect functional duplicates, consciousness neither suddenly vanishes nor gradually fades, so it stays (pp.36-38). Consciousness is an "organizational invariant"; "a simulated rainstorm is not wet", but a simulated computer is a computer (p.39).
- **Identity (§10):**
  - Optimistic vs pessimistic views; destructive uploading as "a form of survival" vs "a form of death" (p.40).
  - Twin argument: identity is "not an organizational invariant" (p.41).
  - Relation to Parfit's teletransportation (fn. 33).
  - Theories of identity: biological, psychological and closest-continuer.
  - 2009 PhilPapers survey: on teletransportation, 38% survival vs 31% death (fn. 35, p.42).
- **Argument from nondestructive uploading (pessimistic):** BioDave and DigiDave. If DigiDave isn't Dave when BioDave survives, why would he be when BioDave is destroyed? (pp.42-43). Replies: closest continuer, or **fission** (Parfit).
- **Argument from gradual uploading (optimistic):** replace 1% a month for 100 months. Otherwise "replacing a single neuron can in effect kill a person"; "everyday neural death may be killing us" (p.44). Continuity of consciousness is "an extremely strong basis" (p.45). Speed it up to hours, minutes, seconds, and finally instant uploading.
- **Pessimist reply:** intermediate cases matter (ship of Theseus) (p.46).
- **His stance:**
  - "My instincts favor optimism here, but as before I cannot be certain" (p.46).
  - "the safest form of uploading is gradual uploading"; he would choose it over "an increasingly slow biological embodiment" (p.47).
  - Hesitant about destructive uploading.
- **Reconstructive uploading** from records (Einstein example); the twin worry (p.48).
- **Further-fact vs deflationary views:**
  - "Edenic survival"; on the deflationary view "uploading is not much worse than waking up" (pp.49-52).
  - "Ordinary survival is not so bad" (p.53).
- **Aside:** he writes about uploading partly to "encourage our successors to reconstruct me, if only to prove me wrong" (p.53).
- **Conclusion:** integrate "By gradual uploading followed by enhancement" (p.53).

**Parfit (1984), §§75-93**
- **§75:**
  - Simple Teletransportation: a Scanner destroys you on Earth; a Replica is made on Mars.
  - Branch-Line Case: the New Scanner leaves you alive with a damaged heart, and you talk to your Replica.
  - "Dying when I know that I shall have a Replica is not quite as bad as, simply, dying" (p.201).
  - Parfit's claim: "being destroyed and Replicated is about as good as ordinary survival" (p.201).
- **§76:** qualitative vs numerical identity (billiard balls) (pp.201-202).
- **§§77-78:**
  - Physical Criterion: enough of the brain, non-branching.
  - Psychological Criterion: connectedness and continuity (overlapping chains), with narrow, wide and widest versions of the cause.
  - Ship analogy and cell replacement (p.204).
  - Artificial-eye analogy: "as good as" seeing (pp.208-209).
- **§79:**
  - Reductionism: identity "just consists in" more particular facts.
  - The club example: an "empty question" (pp.213-214).
  - **Relation R** = "psychological connectedness and/or continuity, with the right kind of cause" (p.215).
  - "Personal identity is not what matters" (p.217).
- **§§80-82:**
  - Quasi-memory: Jane gets Paul's Venice memories (pp.220-222).
  - The subject of experiences: Lichtenberg's "It is thought".
  - Cartesian egos could have been supported by reincarnation evidence, but there is none (pp.227-228).
- **§83:** Williams's surgeon / Napoleon argument against the Psychological Criterion.
- **§§84-86:**
  - Psychological, Physical and Combined Spectra.
  - The Combined Spectrum ends in a Replica of Greta Garbo.
  - A sharp borderline would be unknowable, so Reductionism follows (pp.231-243).
- **§§87-88:**
  - Real split-brain cases: "two separate spheres of consciousness" (p.245).
  - The imagined "Physics Exam", where he divides his mind for ten minutes; the mind as "a river" with separate streams (p.247).
- **§89 My Division:**
  - Each half of his brain goes into one of two triplets' bodies.
  - The four possibilities.
  - "How could a double success be a failure?" (p.256).
  - Best description: "neither of the resulting people will be me" (p.260).
- **§90:**
  - "Nothing is missing. What is wrong can only be the duplication." (p.261)
  - "To regard these as the same is to confuse two with zero" (p.262).
  - U (uniqueness) adds little value.
- **§91:** no criterion of identity can meet both of Williams's requirements.
  - Requirement 1: identity depends only on intrinsic features.
  - Requirement 2: identity can't hang on a trivial fact.
  - The Io example (pp.266-273).
- **§92:** Buddha would have agreed (p.273).
- **§93:** Nagel's brain view, as quoted by Parfit: a replica "would not be me" (p.274). Parfit concedes that many people would accept Nagel's view.

**Schneider (2019), Ch. 5, 6, 8**
- **Ch.5:**
  - "Fusion-optimism" (merging with AI).
  - Four theories of the person: psychological continuity, brain-based materialism, soul theory, no-self view.
  - Patternism (Kurzweil: "I am rather like the pattern that water makes in a stream", as quoted by Schneider).
  - The Software Approach to the Mind.
  - The Transhumanist FAQ says survival only requires "certain information patterns".
- **Ch.6 Mindscan** (Robert Sawyer's novel):
  - Jake is scanned and is still on the table: "this me is still doomed" (Sawyer, as quoted by Schneider).
  - The **reduplication problem**: having the pattern "cannot be sufficient for personal identity".
  - The spacetime-worm fix gives modified patternism, which **rules out uploading**.
  - Middle-range enhancements have an unclear boundary: "the transhumanist developmental trajectory is, for all we know, a technophile's alluring path to suicide."
  - Nanobot / two-brains case.
  - **Metaphysical humility**: public dialogue, pluralism, and "not assume that science alone can answer" these questions.
- **Ch.8:**
  - Kim Suozzi's cryopreservation (Alcor).
  - A mind jumping location "as if by magic"; "stupendous metaphysical luck".
  - The Software View is a **category mistake**, because programs are abstract and minds are concrete and causal.
  - Type vs token (Data from Star Trek): an individual "AI mind is vulnerable to destruction... just as we are"; uploading creates "a different token of the same type".
  - Software-instantiation view is "uninformative".
  - "Uploading, at least as a means of survival, rests on flawed conceptual foundations."
  - Uploading could still have value: preserving a "way of life", space travel, research, digital doubles that may be "sentient beings, deserving to be treated with dignity and respect".

**Ken Liu stories**
- **"The Gods Will Not Be Chained":**
  - A bullied girl, Maddie, is helped by an emoji-speaking presence: her dead father David.
  - His company did a destructive brain scan: "requires destruction of the tissue" (p.149). They wanted to keep only his circuit-design intuition, but "The brain is holonomic... We were arrogant to think that we could isolate the personality away from the technical know-how" (p.150).
  - Other uploaded minds are used as tools.
  - "We've created gods, she thought, and the gods will not be chained." (p.155)
- **"Staying Behind":**
  - "After the Singularity, most people chose to die." (p.157)
  - The uploaded mother's email: "What difference does it make if those electrons are in a brain or silicon chips?" (p.165).
  - The narrator: "This wasn't my mother speaking" (p.165).
  - The daughter: "This is my choice. Not yours." (p.171).
- **"The Gods Will Not Be Slain":**
  - Partial uploads, "no longer quite human, and not entirely artificial" (p.189).
  - Distributed minds; a war between the "gods"; a "stub" of the father; the fork-bomb sacrifice.
  - Ends with "Your cloud-born sister."
- **"The Gods Have Not Died in Vain":**
  - Mist, a "child of the ether": "I do not yearn for something that I never had" (p.239).
  - Maddie: "Life is about embodiment" (p.227).
  - Adam Ever uploads while healthy: "It is me... The only me that matters" (p.242). Maddie answers, "It's not you."
  - The case for leaving scarcity behind vs Maddie's question about whether a disembodied existence is "really living at all" (p.243).

## 2. Key points by theme
- **A. Is the upload conscious?** Chalmers argues yes: functionalism and the neuron-by-neuron replacement argument (§9). The opposing view (biological theorists, Searle) holds that consciousness needs biology. Chalmers admits "we have no idea how a nonbiological system... could be conscious", and adds "we also have no idea how a biological system... could be" (pp.36-37).
- **B. Is it you?** The twin argument shows that identical organisation doesn't mean identity (C p.41). Reduplication, i.e. Mindscan and the bar scene (S ch.6, ch.8). Branch-Line (P §75).
- **C. Gradual vs instant.** Chalmers' 1%-a-month argument and the ship of Theseus. Schneider's worry about where to draw the line in middle-range cases.
- **D. Does identity even matter?** Parfit's Relation R; division is "about as good as ordinary survival" and "two with zero". Chalmers' deflationary reading: uploading is "not much worse than waking up".
- **E. Pattern vs stuff vs program.** Kurzweil's patternism; Schneider's category-mistake argument and type vs token. Chalmers: consciousness is an organizational invariant, but personal identity is not.
- **F. Ethics and politics (Liu, Pantheon premise).**
  - Uploads used as corporate tools ("keep your top engineer as an algorithm", p.149).
  - Consent ("This is my choice. Not yours.").
  - Copies and rights: Chalmers' DigiDave "presumably has some rights" but "not ... BioDave's rights" (p.42); Schneider's "dignity and respect".

## 4. Advocate section (upload = survival / continuity)
- **Chalmers:**
  - Gradual uploading is the safest route and plausibly survival.
  - The continuity-of-consciousness argument.
  - The fading/disappearing-qualia argument for upload consciousness.
  - On the deflationary view, even destructive uploading is "reasonably close to as good as ordinary survival" (p.53).
- **Parfit:**
  - Relation R is what matters, "with any cause" (p.215).
  - Duplication is "two", not "zero".
  - Our cells already change; the ship analogy (p.204).
  - Split-brain cases show consciousness can divide (§87).
- **Hanson (paraphrase only; book not in library):** *The Age of Em* (Oxford UP, 2016) is an economist's detailed forecast of a world of brain emulations ("ems") that can be copied, sped up and run in huge numbers. Use it only as a one-line mention: the optimist side takes uploads seriously enough to do economics about them. Chalmers' bibliography lists Hanson 1994, "If uploads come first", which can be verified from the source.
- **Liu, as evidence for continuity:** the father's love and quirks survive; "The brain is holonomic"; the emoji code only he and Maddie share is a recognisable personal signature (p.143).

## 5. Antagonist section (copy, not you)
- **Schneider:**
  - The reduplication problem (Mindscan).
  - Spatiotemporal continuity: an upload's mind would "jump" across spacetime.
  - The software category mistake.
  - Type vs token.
  - The middle-range boundary problem.
  - Metaphysical humility.
  - Uploading may be useful but is not survival.
- **Chalmers' own pessimist argument:** BioDave and DigiDave; the twin argument. He would hesitate before destructive uploading.
- **Parfit's concessions:** in the Branch-Line Case "it seems clear that he is not me" (p.201); he concedes that many people would accept Nagel's "replica would not be me" (p.274). His best description of division is that "neither... will be me" (p.260).
- **Searle (paraphrase only; article not in library):** "Minds, Brains, and Programs", *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* 3 (1980). The Chinese Room thought experiment: a person in a room follows rules for manipulating Chinese symbols well enough to pass as a Chinese speaker without understanding any Chinese. He concludes that running a program (syntax) is not enough for understanding (semantics), and that minds depend on the causal powers of brains. Chalmers lists Searle among those sympathetic to biological theories (fn. 28). No quotes.
- **Liu:** the mother in "Staying Behind" ("There are worse things than death", p.162); "This wasn't my mother speaking"; Maddie: "It's not you" (p.242).

## 6. Concessions available (for fair debate)
- **The advocate can concede:**
  - Chalmers himself is unsure about destructive uploading.
  - Parfit's view is "hard to believe" (he says so, p.214, p.243).
  - Corporate abuse is real (Liu).
- **The antagonist can concede:**
  - Schneider calls herself broadly transhumanist (ch.8) and finds value in uploads (ch.8).
  - Modified patternism allows gradual biological repair.
  - The gradual-replacement puzzle is hard ("my intuitions crap out", ch.6; don't quote that phrase on air).

## 7. Disputed questions for the episode
1. Would the upload even be conscious?
2. If it's conscious, is it you, or a very convincing copy?
3. Does going slowly (neuron by neuron) change the answer?
4. Does identity even matter (Parfit)?
5. Is your mind a program?
6. Who owns an uploaded mind (Pantheon premise / Liu)?
7. Would you do it, and who gets to choose?
