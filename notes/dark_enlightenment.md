## Key points by theme

### A. Enlightenment reversed and exit (Part 1)
- Enlightenment is by nature progressive and "self-evident", so a reactionary "dark enlightenment" is almost a contradiction in terms. Hayek's "Old Whig" is described as a "reactionary progressive".
- Milestone: Cato Unbound, April 2009 (Patri Friedman, Peter Thiel). Land cites Thiel saying he no longer believes freedom and democracy are compatible. Michael Lind (Salon, Aug 2011) answers that libertarianism is anti-democratic.
- Voice vs. exit is described as the rising alternative, "even more than Equality-vs-Liberty". Patri Friedman calls free exit the only universal human right.
- The undercurrent is "Hobbesian": the dark enlightenment sees "appetites" where the progressive one sees ideals, and asks how the sovereign can be kept from devouring society.
- Hoppe (monarchy vs. democracy): a king owns the capital stock, whereas a democratic caretaker only has its "usufruct", so his exploitation is short-sighted.
- Civilisation is falling **time preference**. Democracy drives time preference up and is therefore close to the negation of civilisation ("Tomorrow might belong to the other team, so it's best to eat it all now").
- Churchill's two remarks on democracy are quoted.
- Moldbug is called the "Sith Lord" of neoreaction. Libertarianism is "Sisyphean"; sovereignty cannot be abolished (Hobbes). The fix is to formalise the state: **neocameralism**, where the state is a business that owns a country, with shares, a board and a CEO, and residents are customers. "No voice, free exit."
- Neocameral transition: buy out the real stakeholders. Map the whole landscape of bribery and lobbying and convert it into shares. The ruling entity mapped this way = **the Cathedral**.
- Historical approximations: Frederick the Great's enlightened absolutism; Hong Kong, Singapore and Dubai (quoted from Moldbug).
- Democracy in antiquity was understood as a decadent phase before tyranny. Today's democracy is a creed: Moldbug's **Universalism**, a "nontheistic Christian sect" in the Calvinist/Puritan line. It asks "how Massachusetts came to conquer the world".
- Founding-father quotes against democracy. In an added note Land himself says the "Franklin" quote is probably a 1992 invention by James Bovard (via Barry Popik).

### B. Democracy as parasite (Parts 2–3, 4e)
- Graeber and Sitrin are quoted to show that the left also sees democracy and capitalism as incompatible.
- Hanke: "democracy" appears in neither the Declaration nor the Constitution. Robin Hanson: progress came from the industrial revolution, not from political movements.
- Schumpeter: capitalism creates a democratic-bureaucratic culture that leads to stagnation. Democracy is a lagging indicator; "Democracy consumes progress". Method: "general parasitology".
- **Feedback:** markets give fast, local feedback. Democracy routes it through a slow central "general will" and insulates people from the consequences of their actions. The reactionary answer is "quarantine", or dis-solidarisation. There is a zombie-apocalypse satire.
- **Greece/EU:** the euro wiped out the information in Greek interest rates ("live like Hellenes and pay like Germans").
- **Part 3:** "Whatever is subsidized is promoted", stated as an iron law. McArdle (The Atlantic) on pension systems and birth rates.
- **Four regimes table (Part 3):**
  - Communist tyranny: growth about 0%, low voice / low exit.
  - Authoritarian capitalism: 5–10%, low / high, "flinty realism".
  - Social democracy: 0–3%, high / high, "sanctimonious dishonesty".
  - Zombie apocalypse.
- **Part 4e:**
  - Modernity is an S-curve. Techno-industrial growth is eaten up by rent-seeking ("demosclerosis"), and Mancur Olson's "distributional coalitions" are invoked.
  - Three prospects:
    - **Modernity 2.0**, with China as the new core and India "too far gone".
    - **Postmodernity**, a new dark age.
    - **Western Renaissance**, which requires a "hard reboot".
  - Wish list: constitutional republicanism or something more anti-political, a radically smaller state, hard money and no central bank, and no fiscal or monetary discretion. Land himself calls it unrealistic short of civilisational collapse.

### C. The Cathedral as religion (Parts 1–4)
- Moldbug's "How Dawkins got pwned": the "optimal memetic parasite". Dawkins is described as a "Protestant/Calvinist/Puritan atheist"; his ancestry is Ranters, Levellers and Quakers. Puritans went to New England and won America's wars, so mainstream thought today descends from them.
- Dawkins' "Zeitgeist" is equated with Providence, and that in turn with "Progress". "Progress for the tick is not progress for the dog."
- Hegel and the state as God. **Tolerance paradox:** when only tolerance is tolerated, "perfect tolerance and absolute intolerance" become indistinguishable. The right to be left alone (17th-century Jews in Amsterdam, Huguenots in London) is set against the right to be heard.
- **"Hate":** a crime plus a heretical intention, and asymmetric because only the right can "hate". Grievance status rewards dysfunction ("Marxo-Calvinism").
- Misesian vs. Darwinian "morbidity" in Moldbug's taxonomy (parasitic, altruistic, symbiotic, malignant).
- Universalism is "a mystery cult of power" (Moldbug, quoted by Land). It is as dependent on the state as malaria is on the mosquito.
- **Part 4:** irony is Moldbug's method, and Moldbug describes himself as a Jacobite. The Declaration's "self-evident" truths are called an act of faith. The New Atheism is "neo-puritan parricide". The "War on Christmas" is satirised.
- **Part 4b:** dialectics as the Cathedral's method: "more dialectics is more politics", and politics always moves to the left. The right-wing refuge is the non-argumentative: "Do your own thing." The show trial is "Hegel for prime-time television".
- **Part 4c:** "The left thrives on dialectics, the right perishes through them." Progressivism has no enemies on the left. The right moves to the centre and the centre moves to the left. Conservatism's role is "to be humiliated".

### D. The race digression (Parts 4–4e) (handle critically)
- **White nationalism.** Moldbug's "sanitized" blog list runs from Lawrence Auster to "Tanstaafl". Land describes white identity politics as an orbit into a "black hole" ending in antisemitic conspiracy, a "werewolf curse" that can only become a monster.
- **The suppression mechanism.** Every liberal "cause war" strengthens what it fights. Suppressing politically incorrect claims produces "data-empowered, web-coordinated, paranoid … werewolves". Land presents this as a warning and a prediction.
- **Moldbug quoted.** "white nationalism would be dangerous if … right". Hitler as the "one degree of separation"; Stalin vs. Hitler.
- **"Hitlerism".** Land claims that Hitler functions as an inverse Messiah, the Antichrist, in a new civil religion ("Church of Sacred Hitlerite Abomination").
- **Part 4a.** East Asian cities are safe at night ("the contrary case is barbarism"). Western cities are "donut"-shaped. **White flight** is the "spontaneous impulse of dark enlightenment", "all exit and no voice". The Derbyshire affair (2012, *The Talk: Nonblack Version*, fired from National Review): Land focuses on Derbyshire's word "despair".
- **Part 4b.**
  - Trayvon Martin is read as a media "morality play" that fell apart.
  - Land calls belief in substantial human equality a "Gnostic delirium". HBD and IQ are cast as the most "troublesome" dimension, and HBD people as "obnoxious", which Land treats with a self-ironic sketch.
  - **Land's own criticism of Derbyshire.** The phrase "an individual black" is called "subtly … menacing". Land relays the critiques by William Saletan (Slate) and Noah Millman: a statistical truth is "an ignorant person's weak substitute for knowledge", and "nobody ever encounters a population". Land calls these points "intellectually substantive".
- **Part 4c.**
  - Race in America is told as an Old Testament (slavery, Exodus, Lincoln) and a New Testament (1960s civil rights, the 1965 immigration act, the Southern Strategy).
  - MLK's dream is read as exit symbolism. Land argues that integration is no exit.
  - Anglosphere (James C. Bennett, *The Anglosphere Challenge*): it settles disagreements "in space, rather than time", through secession and flight. **Cracker Factory:** "Escape is racist".
- **Part 4d.**
  - "Cracker" is a slur for poor Scots-Irish Southerners. "Crackers" also break codes and seals: a crack-up, secession.
  - hbdchick on outbreeding and the Hajnal line. The "paradox of white identity": modernity's success comes from weak ethnic groupishness. "Identity politics is for losers", and Nazism is a logical dead end.
  - Libertarianism is being "crackerized" (Mises Institute in Auburn, AL; Ron Paul newsletters; Rothbard; Hoppe; Tom Palmer's protest).
  - Conclusion: "liberty has no future in the Anglophone world outside the prospect of secession".
- **Part 4e.** The Civil War "cross-coded" liberty into emancipation vs. independence. "Within a cross-coded history, the realization of freedom is indistinguishable from its abolition." Racial code words ("states' rights").

### E. Bionic horizon (Part 4f)
- The digression's theme is the Cathedral as **thought suppression**: what gets suppressed becomes monsters, and "a time of monsters is approaching".
- The central dogma is the SSSM / blank slate (Boas). Simon Blackburn on Pinker: the right likes genes, the left likes culture.
- Land rejects **both** hereditarian determinism and social constructivism. Techno-science is a circuit in which knowing a thing means being able to modify it, and reading the genome becomes editing it.
- **Bionic horizon:** the point at which a population becomes indistinguishable from its technology. Octavia Butler's *Xenogenesis* (the Oankali gene traders).
- John H. Campbell: *Homo autocatalyticus*, "generative evolution", elite groups leaving Homo sapiens as a "living fossil". For racial nationalists Campbell is "the abyss … Think face tentacles". Secession becomes **speciation**. Last sentence: the racial dialectic is trapped in trivialities, and it is "time to move on".

## Links back
- **Patchwork:** Land treats neocameralism, the Cathedral and Universalism as Moldbug's concepts and quotes his definition (a state is a business that owns a country). Moldbug: libertarianism is Sisyphean. Exit runs through both texts.
- **Fanged Noumena:** acceleration, techno-capital as a positive feedback circuit, and the inhuman future reappear in the bionic horizon, in time preference and in Modernity 2.0 (China/Shanghai; Land lives in Shanghai).

## Critics (verified)
- **Elizabeth Sandifer**, *Neoreaction a Basilisk* (Eruditorum Press, 2017). Reads Moldbug, Land and Yudkowsky together and treats the provocations as part of the substance.
- **Scott Alexander**, "The Anti-Reactionary FAQ" (Slate Star Codex, 20 Oct 2013). An empirical response on monarchy, crime and the Cathedral.
- **Benjamin Noys**, *Malign Velocities: Accelerationism and Capitalism* (Zero Books, 2014). Coined the term "accelerationism" in its critical sense (in *The Persistence of the Negative*, 2010). Here he reads accelerationism as a symptom of the misery of labour under capitalism.
- **Andy Beckett**, "Accelerationism: how a fringe philosophy predicted the future we live in" (The Guardian, 11 May 2017). Traces the path from Land's 90s accelerationism to neoreaction.
- **Angela Saini**, *Superior: The Return of Race Science* (Beacon Press, 2019). Critique of HBD and race science.
- **Albert O. Hirschman**, *Exit, Voice, and Loyalty* (1970), from Patchwork. Exit without voice.
- **Internal critics in the text:** William Saletan and Noah Millman on Derbyshire (Part 4b); Michael Lind (Part 1); Tom Palmer (Part 4d).
