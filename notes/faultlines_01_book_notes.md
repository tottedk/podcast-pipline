## 1. Carl Schmitt, *The Concept of the Political* (1932; 1963 foreword) [10 quotes]
The far-right guest's (Elliot's) home text, and the frame the whole episode tests: is the left/right split turning into a friend/enemy grouping?
1. **The criterion.** "The specific political distinction to which political actions and motives can be traced is the distinction between friend and enemy." (PDF p.20)
2. **Intensity, not subject matter.** "The distinction between friend and enemy denotes the extreme intensity of a connection or separation, an association or dissociation" (PDF p.20)
3. **The enemy is not (necessarily) evil.** "The political enemy need not be morally evil; he need not be aesthetically ugly; he need not be an economic competitor—it may even seem advantageous to do business with him. He is just the other, the stranger" (PDF p.20). *Useful contrast*: today's partisans call each other "evil" (Kalmoe & Mason), which by Schmitt's own logic is the moralised, *worse* form of enmity.
4. **Public, not private.** "He is also not a private opponent whom one hates with a feeling of antipathy. The enemy exists only when, at least potentially, one fighting collective of people confronts another fighting collective. The enemy is always a public enemy" (PDF p.22)
5. **hostis vs inimicus.** "The enemy is hostis, not inimicus in the broader sense" (PDF p.22)
6. **The killing clause.** "The terms friend, enemy, and conflict receive and retain their real meaning by the fact that they refer specifically to the real possibility of physical killing. War follows from enmity, for it means the existential negation of another being." (PDF p.25). The hardest line for the Kirk discussion.
7. **Domestic enmity: when party politics becomes everything.** "The equation 'political = party politics' is possible when the idea of a comprehensive political unit (the state) that encompasses all domestic political parties and their antagonisms loses strength and, as a result, domestic antagonisms become more intense than the unified foreign policy" (PDF p.24). Almost a description of affective polarization.
8. **Civil war is included.** "The real and ever-present possibility of conflict always pertains to the concept of the enemy." (PDF p.25; the same passage says the concept refers not only to war between states "but to civil war")
9. **"Humanity" as a weapon.** "Here, with an obvious modification, a saying coined by Proudhon applies: 'Whoever invokes "humanity" wishes to cheat.'" and the invocation can mean "the enemy is to be denied the quality of humanity" (PDF p.42). Elliot uses this against left moralism; Clara turns it on the right's "the left are demons" rhetoric.
10. **1963 foreword: limiting enmity.** "The big problem is the limitation of war, and this is either a cynical game, the organization of a dog fight, or an empty self-deception, unless it is connected on both sides with a relativization of the enmity." (PDF p.14). Even Schmitt wants enmity *relativised*, which is a surprising bridge to Mouffe.
- Extra (1963 foreword, PDF p.8): Schmitt warns against unleashing "revolutionary class or racial enmities which can no longer distinguish between enemy" [and criminal], i.e. against total, moralised enmity.

## 2. Chantal Mouffe, *On the Political* (2005) [9 quotes]
The far-left guest's (Clara's) theory, and the source of Carter's "way out" question. Her thesis: the moralised, consensus-centrist politics that drains real left/right choice is what *produces* antagonism.
1. **Target: the post-political centre.** "I want to challenge this 'post-political' vision. My main target will be those in the progressive camp who accept this optimistic view of globalization and have become the advocates of a consensual form of democracy." (p.1, Introduction)
2. **Against "beyond left and right".** "Their aim is the establishment of a world 'beyond left and right', 'beyond hegemony', 'beyond sovereignty' and 'beyond antagonism'. Such a longing reveals a complete lack of understanding of what is at stake in democratic politics" (p.2)
3. **Consensus is dangerous.** "It is my contention that envisaging the aim of democratic politics in terms of consensus and reconciliation is not only conceptually mistaken, it is also fraught with political dangers." (p.2)
4. **Moralisation: the core diagnosis.** "What is happening is that nowadays the political is played out in the moral register. In other words, it still consists in a we/they discrimination, but the we/they, instead of being defined with political categories, is now established in moral terms. In place of a struggle between 'right and left' we are faced with a struggle between 'right and wrong'." (p.5)
5. **Why that turns into enmity.** "when instead of being formulated as a political confrontation between 'adversaries', the we/they confrontation is visualized as a moral one between good and evil, the opponent can be perceived only as an enemy to be destroyed and this is not conducive to an agonistic treatment." (p.5)
6. **Channels.** "when the channels are not available through which conflicts could take an 'agonistic' form, those conflicts tend to emerge on the antagonistic mode." (p.5)
7. **Adversaries, not enemies.** "agonism is a we/they relation where the conflicting parties, although acknowledging that there is no rational solution to their conflict, nevertheless recognize the legitimacy of their opponents. They are 'adversaries' not enemies." (p.20)
8. **The task.** "We could say that the task of democracy is to transform antagonism into agonism." (p.20)
9. **The centre feeds the populist right (Denmark is named).** "It is obviously impossible to attribute the growing success of right-wing populist parties in Belgium, Denmark, Switzerland, the Netherlands, Norway, Italy and France ... to the absence in those countries of a critical relationship with their past." (p.65) and "in each case a consensus at the centre had been established, which did not allow voters to make of a real choice between significantly different policies" (p.66, "make of a real choice" sic). The **Danish hook**: Denmark has had a broad centre (SVM) government since 2022. Does Mouffe predict a backlash?
- Extra (p.75): "There is, in my view, a direct link between the weakening of the political frontier characteristic of the adversarial model and the 'moralization' of politics."

## 3. Lilliana Mason, *Uncivil Agreement* (2018) [8 quotes] (OCR-cleaned)
Why the anger outruns the policy disagreement: social sorting turns party into a "mega-identity".
1. **Social polarization defined.** "Rather than simply disagreeing over policy outcomes, we are increasingly blind to our commonalities, seeing each other only as two teams fighting for a trophy. Social polarization is defined by prejudice, anger, and activism on behalf of that prejudice and anger." (p.4)
2. **The key line for "why the rise".** "These phenomena are increasing quickly — more quickly, in fact, than the level of our policy disagreements. We act like we disagree more than we really do." (p.4)
3. **Identity, not reasons.** "our conflicts are largely over who we think we are rather than over reasoned differences of opinion." (p.4)
4. **Mega-identity.** "Partisanship can now be thought of as a mega-identity, with all the psychological and behavioral magnifications that implies" (p.14; the OCR reads "as.a.mega-identity")
5. **Winning over outcomes (the Tajfel experiments).** "it is the winning that seems more important to them" (p.11, quoting Tajfel et al. 1971, 172)
6. **Threat makes preference into hostility.** "Under circumstances of perceived threat or competition, however, the preference for the ingroup can lead to outright hostility toward the outgroup, particularly when the competition is a zero-sum game" (p.12)
7. **Cross-cutting ties lost.** "this new alignment has degraded the cross-cutting social ties that once allowed for partisan compromise." (p.6). The civil-war link: "Selway (2011) finds that cross-cutting identities significantly reduce the risk of civil war in a country, while the alignment of ethnic and religious identities speeds the onset of civil war." (p.128)
8. **The verdict.** "when a nation changes from one made up of many cross-cutting identities to one built on socially segregated parties, the result is an electorate that is, on average, more angry, excitable, and active than it was before the social shift." (p.141)
- Anchor story (pp.1-2): Sherif's 1954 **Robbers Cave** experiment, the Rattlers vs the Eagles: "The Eagles burned the Rattlers' flag." (p.1). A great story for Carter.
- Epigraph (p.127, Mason quoting Ian Buruma via Kalyvas 2006): "Identity is what gets the blood boiling, what makes people do unspeakable things to their neighbors." This is Buruma's line, so attribute it to him if used.

## 4. Nathan Kalmoe & Lilliana Mason, *Radical American Partisanship* (2022) [9 quotes]
The data core of "is it a war?": moral disengagement, "evil", tolerance of violence, including **assassination**, which ties directly to Kirk. Note the data runs 2017 to Feb 2021, before Kirk.
1. **Scope.** "Most Americans rejected partisan violence, but a substantial minority expressed support, as many as one in five. Providing some reassurance, however, only a fraction of those who endorse violence have lethal attacks in mind." (p.7)
2. **"Evil" and "threat" are mainstream.** "By then, nearly three-quarters of partisans viewed opponents as a national threat and nearly 60 percent considered them to be 'evil.'" (p.7, Feb 2021)
3. **Asymmetry by 2021.** "Twenty percent of Republicans endorsed partisan violence 'today,' compared with one in eight Democrats." (pp.7-8) and "these final measures show Republicans unambiguously more radical than Democrats on all dimensions." (p.8)
4. **But symmetric in 2017.** "12 percent of Republicans and 18 percent of Democrats approved of violence at least 'a little' if the other party were to win the 2020 presidential election. That party difference is not statistically significant." (p.47). This gives **both** guests ammunition.
5. **Assassination, the Kirk question.** "'How much do you feel it is justified for [Own party] to kill opposing political leaders to advance their political goals these days?' Twelve percent of Republicans and 11 percent of Democrats said assassinations carried out by their party were at least 'a little bit' justified." (p.69)
6. **Scaled up.** "Generalizing to the population of American partisans means roughly twenty million who endorse assassinating US leaders." (p.69)
7. **Not (yet) a war.** "We see nothing today like the levels of partisan violence that plagued the distant American past. Nonetheless, levels of partisan moral disengagement and support for partisan violence have clearly grown, and ethno-partisan alignments of political parties suggest more conflict ahead." (p.69)
8. **Leaders matter (the practical fix).** "antiviolence messages from Biden and Trump significantly reduced support for partisan violence in 2019 and 2020 tests, particularly for violence tied to election losses, with the greatest reduction among the strong partisans who are most inclined to support violence." (p.9). And the reverse: "If group leaders and members engage in, encourage, or ignore violence ... they grow increasingly likely to grow more violent as a group." (p.26)
9. **Moral disengagement.** "Moral disengagement cushions the ego when people contemplate behaviors that hurt other people, both before the act and after as a post hoc rationalization" (p.35)
- Also: the book calls the American Revolution "partisan warfare" (p.13), and says "there is no question Donald Trump's postelection rhetoric caused the January 2021 attack on the Capitol" (p.9; the authors' claim). Elliot will attack this as the authors' bias.
- Who the radicals are (p.8): "radical partisans have stronger psychological attachments to their party, and they tend to have aggressive personality traits"; "ideology has little effect".
