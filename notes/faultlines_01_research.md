## (c) Practical application / real-world cases
- **Political violence 2024-25 (US):** the assassination of Charlie Kirk (10 Sep 2025, Utah Valley University; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Charlie_Kirk); the shootings of Minnesota legislators (14 Jun 2025, Rep. Melissa Hortman and her husband killed; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_shootings_of_Minnesota_legislators); the killing of UnitedHealthcare CEO Brian Thompson (4 Dec 2024; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_of_Brian_Thompson) and the online celebration of the suspect. Violence and its fans exist on both sides, which pairs well with Kalmoe & Mason.
- **January 6, 2021 Capitol attack** (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_6_United_States_Capitol_attack): the contested meaning of "insurrection" vs "protest".
- **Denmark:** the assault on PM Mette Frederiksen at Kultorvet, Copenhagen (7 Jun 2024; Wikipedia "2024 in Denmark"); Rasmus Paludan/Stram Kurs and the Quran burnings; the **Folketing ban on burning the Quran (Dec 2023)**, where the parliament was split (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasmus_Paludan). The Danish consensus model, with a broad SVM centre government since 2022, works as the counterexample.
- **Practice:** what the depolarisation research says works (Voelkel 2024; Perception Gap), and why Bail 2018 suggests "just talk to each other" can backfire.

## (d) Object of focus: 3 options
1. **(Recommended) The assassination of Charlie Kirk, 10 Sep 2025, and the reactions to it.** It speaks directly to "is it becoming a war?". It tests both guests: the far right reads it as proof of a left war, the far left points to Hortman and Jan 6, and both have to answer for their own side's online celebrants. It brings in Schmitt (enemy), Hobbes (breakdown of the referee) and Kalmoe & Mason (tolerance of violence). Handle it carefully: facts from sourced reports only, no speculation about the motive beyond what is established, and no invented quotes from Kirk or others.
2. **Denmark's Quran-burning ban (Folketing, Dec 2023).** The Nordic angle. It splits along unusual lines (free speech vs public order and security vs "capitulation"), and it lets the guests fight about Mill, Paludan, Islam and immigration on home ground. Thomas's own country, so it feels close.
3. **A data artifact: the "feeling thermometer" chart** (ANES partisan warmth toward the other party, falling since the 1980s; Iyengar et al. 2012/2019), **or the More in Common perception-gap quiz**. Carter "shows" it to the guests. Less dramatic but the most analytical: "we hate each other more but don't disagree more".

## Carter's question set (draft; 6-9 get used, 2-3 must evolve live)
1. Cold-open bait: "Is this already a war, or are we just very rude to each other on the internet?"
2. Schmitt says politics *is* friend versus enemy. Is he describing us, or is he a warning?
3. Mouffe says turn enemies into adversaries. What would you have to accept about the other side for that to work, and would you?
4. The research says we don't disagree much more on policy than in the 90s, we just hate each other more. Why? (Iyengar, Mason)
5. Perception-gap quiz: "How many [other side] do you think believe X?" Carter reveals the real numbers. How wrong are you about each other?
6. Who started it: elites, the media business model, algorithms, the economy, immigration, the culture war?
7. The object of focus (e.g. Kirk, 10 Sep 2025): what did your side's reaction say about your side?
8. Political violence: is it ever justified for your cause? Where exactly is your line? (Kalmoe & Mason)
9. Is Denmark the counterexample (consensus, a broad centre government), or just late? The Quran-burning law: order, or capitulation?
10. Burke vs Marx: is this about preserving vs overturning, or about who gets to count as "the people"?
11. What do you fear most if the other side wins completely?
12. Personal: who in your life do you no longer speak to because of politics?
