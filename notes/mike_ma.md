# Mike Ma: *Harassment Architecture* and *Gothic Violence* (research note)

**CONTENT WARNING.** Both books are transgressive fiction. They contain slurs, hatred of ethnic, religious, sexual and gender groups, misogyny, sexual violence, suicide, and first-person fantasies and narrations of mass violence and terror. This note describes them analytically and does not reproduce slurs or operational detail. Nothing here is known about the author beyond what the two books themselves print.

## Editions used (PDF scans with OCR errors, printed page numbers cited)
- **Harassment Architecture**, Mike Ma, Raleigh NC, 2019, self-published ("Mike Ma"), ISBN 9781795641494 per the file name. 158 PDF pages. Cover title: *A Scattered Look at Harassment Architecture*; running title *Harassment Architecture*. Cited as **HA p.N**.
- **Gothic Violence**, Mike Ma, Raleigh NC, 2021, independently published. 197 PDF pages. The file name gives ISBN 9788511367768, the printed last page reads 9798511367767: a discrepancy, so use the printed one with care. Cited as **GV p.N**.
- **How I read them (honest scope):** HA pages 1-45 read in full, the rest by a full heading map plus every passage containing "accelerat" plus targeted passages (about 12). GV by full heading map, keyword scans and about 12 targeted chapters (opening, cyber chapter, ritual chapter, an essay chapter, the secession finale, the closing essay, author page, endnotes, addenda). This is a skim, not a verbatim reading of every page; claims below are limited to what was actually read.

## 1. Harassment Architecture: outline
- **Form:** roughly 100 short pieces (estimated from a heading scan) ("fragments"), most one to five pages, in a first-person voice. Two printed "warnings" up front (p.5, p.7): the first says the contents "reflect nothing upon the author himself" and are "purely fiction"; the second says whoever expects "coherent plot or structure" bought the wrong book. Dedication to Alex Kazemi and initials (p.7). Chapters the author values most are marked with a small "x".
- **Opening piece (pp.8-13):** the narrator harasses a young woman at a traffic light, causes a car crash and drives off, then daydreams a mass shooting at a concert that turns out to be a daydream. Establishes the tone: deadpan, self-mocking, cruel, with the line between fantasy and act deliberately unstable.
- **Middle (pp.14-120):** diaristic scenes (cafe, barber, gym, bars, New York, New Jersey), rants about cities, work, irony, social media, fashion, diets and hatred of groups; nostalgia for an imagined 1990s (p.14-16); yearning to live in nature and reject industrial civilisation (p.22-24); sin, guilt, Christianity and God as a recurring register (p.25, p.40); a long second-person piece addressed to a young woman, "Sophia", mixing stalking, humiliation, sexual violence and a suicide fantasy (pp.41-44).
- **Acceleration pieces:** p.37 ("Yes, this is a form of acceleration", following a racist conspiracy claim); pp.49-51 a piece about criminals and style; pp.86-87 "Accelerate the world, decelerate your tribe"; p.121 "I am not a satirist."; p.136 a "terror spree" scene after an "Author's Note" saying none of it is imagined; pp.148-150 a recruitment speech in a wood that names the listener "an engine of chaos, an accelerationist. You architect harassment."
- **End matter:** "About the author" (p.152) with press-blurb style quotes and a biography as printed; footnotes (p.154); two mock political ads for a "Pine Tree Party" urging chaos and vandalism; a closing page on gluten and mental illness with quoted statements.
- **Recurring features:** an "editor" who interrupts the text ("Extremely off-topic", p.29); parenthetical disclaimers ("Please note: Do not do any of these things", p.28); health-purity obsessions (raw meat, seed oils, gluten); anti-urban, anti-technology, anti-"honest work" tirades (p.38-40).

## 2. Gothic Violence: outline
- **Front matter:** dedication "to Jesus Christ" and to musicians and initials; a legal disclaimer: the story is fiction, the author "does not intend to incite a revolution" and "does not condone the use of force, violence, or weapons", and asks readers to stop adding him to watchlists. A line adds that a digital copy "is stolen".
- **Form:** about 55 titled chapters of one to six pages, more novelistic and gothic in diction than HA, in the first person; a loose arc rather than a plot. OCR is noisy.
- **Opening (pp.9-11):** an "oracle of the Downward Pointed Earth" (Lucretia) visits the narrator with a deck of "terror cards" and a ticket to Los Angeles. The narrator states his premise: to "usher in the return of authenticity", and says a full-scale societal collapse "doesn't happen on its own" and is "needed for the plan to work" (p.11).
- **Middle:** Los Angeles scenes, beach and surf gangs, car culture; a chapter in which a crew uses banks of old computers to disrupt companies and flood executives' inboxes with junk (p.31; described here only at that level); a chapter proposing blood sacrifice as a ritual duty when building on land and "ritual slaughter" of "undesirables" (pp.45-47); an essay chapter that treats transgender people as an "inevitable product" of industry and democracy and speaks of being "supportive" in order to hasten decline (p.79; this is hostile, not supportive, in effect); a chapter arguing there are no neutrals and that ambiguity "must be buried grave deep" (p.107); anti-modern and anti-technology polemic: "the further we wind things back... the better life gets" (p.69); Jewish, Palestinian, Latino and other characters appear in a mix of caricature and slurs.
- **Finale (pp.163-170):** "The sedition of Florida": the narrator and thousands of followers secede from the "mainland" as "New Atlantis". Those who refuse the terms "are quite literally pushed into the sea" by paramilitaries (p.165); a federal helicopter is shot down by teenagers with grenades (p.165); the new state bans modern tools, removes the grid, cell towers and internet, and allows only live music (p.169).
- **Closing essay (p.171):** states that the book is "no doomsday tome", that "violence is a vaccine. It works, but at the cost of everything", that "there is nothing dark about this", and "authenticity is owed to us all". The last pages (pp.173-175) turn to blood, land, sons, women as bearers of sons, and God.
- **Back matter:** "About the author" (p.177) in third person, including that *Harassment Architecture* "was banned from circulation within the United States prison system" and that a Department of Homeland Security publication called him a "violent extremist", quoting his novel as a source, followed by "Is Mike Ma the first author to be designated a threat... for a fiction satire? Perhaps." **These are the book's own claims; they were not verified.** Endnotes (p.179): "I leave no sources for any claims made in this book"; references include Nietzsche's *Antichrist*, Fishwick's *Faust Revisited*, Fomenko, Miles Mathis, Hoffman. A "Nutrition addendum" (after p.179) gives diet advice ("I am not a doctor").

## 3. Key themes (what the texts actually do)
**A. "Accelerationism" as the narrator's self-label (HA).** Acceleration is explained at HA p.87 as "causing problems the right way, the smart way... that keeps you out of jail", and "to lean into" the collapse, not toward "a better world... not immediately at least": "We are leaning into the collapse. We are pushing for the ignition of cleansing fire." The narrator is "acceleration and... the reaction", so "nobody knows who's doing what". At p.150: "an engine of chaos, an accelerationist". So the books use the word in a **collapse-hastening, violent** sense, not in the economic or technological sense of Land or of left accelerationism.
**B. The aesthetics of violence (HA pp.49-51).** The piece argues that criminals are judged by their looks and style; "Violence without good story or style is barbaric at best"; "Maximize looks to maximize crime, and in that, accelerate better. Consider it crimemaxing." It names real attackers as idolised; the book's wording is that the fascination "isn't purely aesthetic".
**C. Anti-nihilism, anti-irony, sincerity (both books).** HA p.23: "Everything is pretentious when everyone is a nihilist"; "Sincerity is dead or laughed at." HA p.27: "I want to care, more, again." GV p.11: "usher in the return of authenticity". GV p.171: "Authenticity is owed to us all." The books diagnose irony as a trap and prescribe commitment, but commitment to violence and exclusion.
**D. Anti-modernity and neo-primitivism.** HA p.22: the Industrial and Agricultural revolutions "have been a disaster for the human race". GV p.69: "the further we wind things back... the better life gets"; GV p.169: no grid, no phone lines, no internet. HA p.86 heading: "Accelerate the world, **decelerate** your tribe." **Tension:** the books' "acceleration" aims at a technology-free return.
**E. Collapse as necessity and as a cleansing event.** GV p.11, GV p.171; HA p.87 "cleansing fire"; HA p.24-25 the drunk-driving image of civilisation heading for a wreck, in which the narrator watches from another car.
**F. Blood, ritual, sin.** GV pp.45-47 (sacrifice as duty); HA p.40, p.136 (sin and punishment, Satan, Christ); closing pages of GV.
**G. Hatred of named groups as structure, not decoration.** Across both books, the targets (women, ethnic and religious minorities, LGBT people, immigrants, city dwellers, "the working class") recur in the rants, the scenes, the plans, and the finale's expulsions. It is not confined to one narrator in one scene.
**H. Fiction shield and its erosion.** Pro-fiction signals: HA p.5 ("purely fiction"), HA p.28 ("Do not do any of these things"), GV disclaimer, HA p.154 ("lots of this book is me making fun of myself. I am also serious as can be, in certain sections."), the "editor" interruptions (HA p.29), HA p.121 ("I am not a satirist"). Erosion signals: the HA "Author's Note" p.136 ("none of it is imagined... very real, very deliberate action"), the political ads at the end of HA, GV p.171's direct address ("violence is a vaccine"), GV p.177's biography.
**I. Purity of the body.** Diets, gluten, raw meat, seed oils, sun worship (HA, GV addendum): a bodily "authenticity" that mirrors the political one.
**Note on vocabulary:** a keyword scan of the GV text found no occurrence of "accelerat" (the scan is OCR-based, so a few misses are possible); the collapse idea is there without the word.
**J. Gothic style.** Black sun, oracles, graves, marble, ritual, guillotines, Atlantis (GV): gothic as aesthetic of doom, nature and blood.

## 5. Advocate section: the fanatic's strongest case (what a committed reader can truly cite)
1. **Diagnosis of irony and nihilism.** The books say out loud what many readers feel: that irony and cynicism have eaten sincerity (HA pp.23, 27). A reader can honestly find that diagnosis sharp.
2. **Acceleration as a view of history.** The idea that the world is a runaway process which cannot be slowed, only ridden, matches the Land-lineage reading: capital as an impersonal, self-accelerating force (Land, Fanged Noumena; Deleuze and Guattari's "go further" line, as quoted by Noys and Fisher). The fanatic can claim the books are the only fiction that takes this seriously.
3. **Form as argument.** Fragmentary, abrasive, parody-and-sincerity mixed; the books refuse comfort and refuse to tell the reader what to feel. HA p.154 explicitly mixes mockery and seriousness.
4. **Disclaimers and genre.** Transgressive fiction has a long history of extreme first-person voices; every text carries disclaimers; HA p.28's "Do not do any of these things."
5. **The "persecuted author" argument.** GV p.177 says the book was cited by a US agency. If true, it would show institutions reading fiction as a threat. Unverified: it is the book's claim.
6. **Anti-technocracy, anti-consumer culture.** The books attack work, screens, diets and urban sameness, which many readers feel too.

## 6. Antagonist section: the critical case, with sources (all paraphrased, no quotes)
- **Humanist / victim ethics (primary voice).** Taken from what the books do: the targets are real categories of people; the narrations treat harm to victims as points scored (HA p.136, drivers and passengers; HA pp.41-44); the finale expels people into the sea (GV p.165). "Fiction" does not remove the question of who is hurt by the aesthetic, or by readers who take the "instructions" seriously. Researchers on violent extremism use the term "militant accelerationism" for milieus that hope to hasten societal collapse by violence; the books' own words match that pattern, though the text does not say whether the author belongs to any milieu.
- **Benjamin Noys** coined "accelerationism" as a critical label in *The Persistence of the Negative* (Edinburgh UP, 2010), and developed the critique in *Malign Velocities: Accelerationism and Capitalism* (Zero Books, 2014). Established points: accelerationism is a fantasy that radicalising capital's own tendencies leads out of capital; it risks affirming what it claims to oppose; it is attracted to speed, intensity and catastrophe as spectacle. He argues instead for negation, not intensification. (Verified via the introduction of *Persistence* and the blurb and preface of *Malign Velocities*.)
- **Mark Fisher.** In "Terminator vs Avatar" (in *#Accelerate: The Accelerationist Reader*, Urbanomic, 2014, ed. Mackay and Avanessian) he takes accelerationism seriously but rejects Land's picture of capital as an inhuman force that discards humans, and argues for a left accelerationism that harnesses productive forces. In *Capitalist Realism* he diagnoses "reflexive impotence" and cynical irony, which overlaps with the books' diagnosis but leads to opposite remedies (collective politics, care about mental health). In *Ghosts of My Life* he coined "the slow cancellation of the future", which cuts against the claim that the culture is accelerating.
- **Srnicek and Williams.** "#Accelerate: Manifesto for an Accelerationist Politics" (2013) and *Inventing the Future* (Verso, 2015): they criticise "folk politics" (localism, immediacy, withdrawal) and propose to repurpose technology and infrastructure, e.g. towards post-work and automation. This is the **opposite** of the books' New Atlantis, which scraps the grid.
- **Textual critique.** (i) The books call themselves accelerationist while prescribing de-modernisation; (ii) the books' fiction shield is weakened by their own author's notes (HA p.136), the political ads, and direct address (GV p.171); (iii) the "authenticity" they praise is exclusionary (GV p.165); (iv) the "violence is a vaccine" claim is unargued.

## 7. Why this counter-guest
The books are not an economic theory. Their "acceleration" is a **moral and aesthetic stance towards violence**, aimed at identifiable groups. A left-accelerationist or Noys-style voice alone would argue about speed and capital while the books' main problem is who is harmed and how it is dressed up. So the counter-guest is a **humanist ethics voice centred on victims**, who brings **Fisher and Noys** as the intellectual backbone (the accelerationist tradition is real, and the books misuse or abandon it) and **Srnicek and Williams** as a contrast (a technology-embracing left that would reject New Atlantis). This keeps the debate fair: the fanatic gets the diagnosis, the theory of history and the literary-form defence; the counter-guest gets the victims, the tradition's own critics, and the text's contradictions.

3. Is it acceleration at all, if the books want to wind the clock back?
4. Does style make violence forgivable?
5. Is nihilism the real enemy?
6. Is collapse a strategy or a fantasy?
